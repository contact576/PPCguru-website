A Toronto service business may see “Google Search Partners” in its campaign settings and wonder whether those clicks are helping. The checkbox is easy to change. The harder part is deciding from **qualified enquiries and booked work**, rather than from click volume alone.

**Short answer:** Search partners extend eligible Search ads beyond Google's own search properties. In Google Ads, use the **Network (with search partners)** segment to compare performance, then match the resulting enquiries to your CRM. Keep the setting only if its reach supports your actual lead and booking goals. Google explains the network in [About the Google Search Network](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722047?hl=en) and the comparison segment in [Use segments in your tables](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2454072?hl=en).

## What are Google Search Partners?

Google's Search Network includes Google search properties and partner sites that show search ads. Depending on the partner, an ad may appear with search results, site directories, product pages or other search-related placements ([Google Ads Help: Search Network](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722047?hl=en)). Search partners are a separate choice from the **Display Network**; check both settings when reviewing where a Search campaign can run.

Google says Search partners are included by default when a Search campaign is created and can be removed in campaign settings. Their inclusion may expand reach, but more reach is useful only if the extra enquiries fit the business. A hypothetical plumbing company serving Toronto and Mississauga should judge the network by serviceable jobs and answered calls, not by the cheapest click.

## Find the setting and the right report

For an existing Search campaign, open its **Networks** settings and look for **Include Google search partners**. Google documents that unchecking this option disables partner delivery for that campaign ([Google Ads Help: Search Network](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722047?hl=en)). Record the current setting before making changes.

Then use the **Network (with search partners)** segment in a Google Ads statistics table or Report Editor. Google says this segment separates Google Search, search partners and Display Network performance ([Google Ads Help: table segments](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2454072?hl=en)). Look at a period long enough to include normal lead follow-up and booking delays. Note each campaign's spend, clicks and reported conversions by network.

Do not merge all campaigns into one verdict. A search partner may perform differently for emergency repair, planned renovation and clinic appointments. Compare similar service lines and location targets. ## Bring the CRM into the decision

A form submission or phone call can be counted in Google Ads even when it never becomes a suitable job. For each network, compare:

| Measure | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Spend and clicks | Shows the scale of the test |
| New enquiries | Shows initial response |
| Qualified enquiries | Removes out-of-area and wrong-service leads |
| Booked appointments or jobs | Connects marketing to work accepted by the business |
| Follow-up time | Reveals whether one source is getting slower handling |

Use your existing definition of a qualified lead across both networks. If one campaign receives too few partner clicks or qualified leads to draw a useful comparison, leave the result as uncertain and keep gathering data. Do not invent a universal threshold for “good” partner performance. See our [lead-quality tracking guide](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/google-ads-lead-quality-tracking-booked-jobs) for a clear lead-stage model.

For example, a hypothetical HVAC campaign could have lower cost per click on search partners but fewer booked service visits per dollar. Another campaign could see similar booking quality on both networks. Those examples illustrate why **cost per booked job** is more useful than click price by itself; they are not PPC Guru client results.

## Change one setting, then document the outcome

If the partner segment shows meaningful spend and consistently poor fit, consider turning partners off for that campaign. Record the change date, the previous performance period and any simultaneous edits to keywords, geography or bidding. If you change several things at once, you will have less evidence about what caused a later shift.

If partner leads are suitable and your team can handle the volume, there may be no reason to remove the extra reach. Keep monitoring because search demand, service coverage and follow-up capacity can change. Google notes that partner-site clicks do not affect Google Search Quality Score ([Google Ads Help: Search Network](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722047?hl=en)); Quality Score is not the reason to switch them off.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Search Partners the same as the Display Network?

No. Search partners are part of the Search Network; Display is a separate network. Review both settings in the campaign rather than assuming “Search campaign” means only Google Search ([Google Ads Help: Google Network](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1752334?hl=en)).

### Can I turn off Search Partners?

Yes. In the Search campaign's Networks settings, uncheck **Include Google search partners** and save. Google's [Search Network guide](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722047?hl=en) gives the current steps.

### What should I compare first?

Start with spend, qualified enquiries and booked jobs by network. Allow enough time for leads to be assessed. If the data is thin, describe the result as uncertain and continue monitoring instead of making a claim from a handful of clicks.
