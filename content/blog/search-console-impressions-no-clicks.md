Search Console shows impressions but no clicks. It is tempting to rewrite every title tag tonight. For a Toronto service business, that may miss the real issue: the page could be appearing low on the results page, matching the wrong query, or competing with a stronger page on your own site. PPC Guru uses this five-check diagnosis to decide which problem to solve first. If your visitors *are* clicking but not enquiring, our separate guide to [traffic rising while leads fall](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/website-traffic-up-leads-down-toronto) addresses that later stage.

**The short answer:** Start with the query and its landing page, not sitewide CTR. Filter Search Console to a meaningful country, date range, page and query; then inspect position, search intent and the live search result. Improve the page's relevance if it answers the wrong question. Improve its title and description only when the page is already a plausible result that people are overlooking.

## First, what does the report actually say?

An impression is a Search Console measurement of a result being shown; a click is a visit from the result; CTR is clicks divided by impressions. Average position is an average of ranking observations, not a fixed rank for every user. Google explains these definitions and notes that its performance data has filtering and privacy limits ([Search Console metric definitions](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7042828?hl=en), [Google's performance-data explanation](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/10/performance-data-deep-dive)).

That matters because 500 impressions and zero clicks can describe very different situations. A page seen for an irrelevant informational term is not the same as a high-intent service page consistently appearing near the top for a term the business wants.

## Five checks, in the order that saves the most wasted work

| Check | What to inspect | Likely decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Query | Is it a question, a service search, a brand search or something unrelated? | Ignore irrelevant demand; answer the intended question if it fits your business. |
| 2. Page | Which URL earned the impression, and is it the page that should own that query? | Strengthen or reassign content before changing snippets. |
| 3. Position and result layout | Is the result plausibly visible for that query, device and location? | Work on relevance and authority if the page is far back; do not diagnose a CTR problem from rank alone. |
| 4. Search appearance | Does the actual title and snippet make the answer and audience clear? | Test clearer wording when the page is already competitive. |
| 5. Business value | Would a click from this query become a suitable enquiry? | Prioritize qualified intent over vanity CTR. |

### Check 1: isolate a query a customer might actually use

In Search Console's Performance report, select a useful recent period and filter to Canada if that is your market. Inspect the query table, then look at the page receiving the impression. Segment branded and non-branded searches when available. Avoid treating the site's combined CTR as a diagnosis; it blends navigational brand searches with very different discovery searches.

### Check 2: make sure the right page is showing

Suppose “commercial roof repair Toronto” shows your general home page instead of the roofing service page. The first question is not whether the home page's title is catchy. Ask whether the service page can be accessed, has a clear offer and receives relevant internal links. If two URLs alternately receive impressions for the same query, inspect whether they answer the same intent; the fix may be page differentiation or consolidation, not another article.

The example is illustrative. For important pages that do not appear at all, use our [Toronto website indexing guide](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/google-not-indexing-website-toronto) rather than treating an absence of impressions as a CTR issue.

### Check 3: read position with caution

A page averaging far below the top results will often collect impressions without many clicks. That does not mean every low CTR is caused by position, or that a reported average of 8 is the position every customer saw. Device, location, date and query mix can change the picture. Look at the exact search result from the target market when possible and compare the result types: service pages, directories, map results, guides or answer boxes.

If competing results solve a different task, improve the page's task fit. A service page cannot satisfy a detailed “how much does an SEO audit include?” question merely by repeating the keyword.

### Check 4: rewrite the snippet only when it is the bottleneck

When the correct page appears prominently for a relevant query yet gets weak clicks, read the title as a buyer would. Does it name the service and Toronto/GTA relevance? Does it distinguish this page from other agency claims? Is it accurate? A clear page title and a useful on-page introduction give Google better material to present, although Google may generate the displayed title or snippet differently from your metadata ([Google title-link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link), [snippet guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)).

Change one priority page at a time. Keep a dated note of the old and new wording, then compare like-for-like queries and periods. Do not claim a lift from a tiny sample or a week with different demand.

### Check 5: choose the business outcome

A question article can draw useful clicks without becoming a lead immediately. A service page can draw fewer clicks but better enquiries. Decide what role the page plays before declaring it underperforming. For PPC Guru's commercial work, qualified enquiries matter more than sitewide CTR; our [SEO services page](https://ppcguru.ca/services/seo) is the appropriate next step if diagnosis points to a broader site problem.

## When should you leave a low-CTR query alone?

Leave it alone when the query is unrelated to what you sell, the impression count is too small to interpret, or the current page serves its intended audience well but the phrase is incidental. Do not retarget every low-click phrase. That approach can dilute a page's answer and create overlaps with stronger content.

For a quick review, select five commercially relevant queries, record the URL, the intended page, current on-page answer, search result type and likely next fix. If the same mismatch appears repeatedly, you have a useful SEO priority. If the report is a mix of unrelated low-volume queries, you may not yet have a systemic problem.
