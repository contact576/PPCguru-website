> **Last reviewed: October 14, 2026. Method:** PPC Guru’s checklist assesses a monthly SEO retainer through its priorities, implemented work, observed evidence and next decision. It is a buyer’s evaluation tool—not a promise of rankings, traffic or leads.

Monthly SEO services should create a visible learning cycle: choose the most important search problem, ship the work, inspect the evidence and decide what to do next. For a Toronto or GTA business, a useful retainer makes it possible to trace the business priority to a specific URL, owner and next action—not just a dashboard full of green charts.

> **Direct answer:** A monthly SEO retainer should include prioritisation, implementation, measurement and clear ownership—not the same task list every month. Ask to see the pages changed, why they were selected, what the evidence means and what will happen next.

## What should monthly SEO services include?

The mix should change as the site and market change. A retainer may cover technical fixes, service-page improvements, local-search work, content refreshes or conversion friction, but each task should have a reason and a record of what happened.

| Area | Work that may matter this month | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategy | Select the service, audience, URL and search problem to address | A short priority list and why it won |
| Technical SEO | Resolve indexing, rendering, duplication, linking or performance blockers | Affected URLs, implementation status and validation |
| On-page and content | Improve the page that owns an intent; add helpful internal links | Before/after URL notes and live date |
| Local SEO | Improve profile accuracy, location relevance and service-area clarity | Changes made and available local-performance signals |
| Authority | Earn or correct relevant references where useful | Source, relevance, destination and status |
| Measurement | Review query mix, page performance and qualified conversions | Definitions, known limits and change over time |
| Reporting | Convert evidence into the next decision | Shipped work, risks, learning and next priorities |

**Scope check:** ask what the agency implements directly, which tasks require your developer or subject expert, and how deferred work returns to the priority list.

## How monthly SEO work should change over time

> **Direct answer:** Month one should establish a baseline and remove urgent blockers. The next 60–90 days should test a small number of high-value page, local or technical priorities. A mature program keeps monitoring but changes its work as evidence, business priorities and site constraints change.

| Stage | Useful focus | What a Toronto business should receive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 30 days | Access, baseline, crawl/indexing review, priority-page mapping and urgent fixes | Named priorities, dependencies, owner and implementation plan |
| Days 31–90 | Improve the highest-value service or local opportunity; resolve the clearest blocker | URL-level changes, a comparison period and the next test |
| Ongoing | Refresh declining pages, improve conversion paths, support new services and monitor risks | A changing priority list—not a recycled activity quota |

This does not mean every program needs three months before work is visible. It means a credible plan separates diagnosis, implementation and interpretation instead of presenting them as one monthly deliverable.

## Use an evidence ledger, not a dashboard dump

| Planned priority | Change shipped | Signal observed | Commercial signal | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Improve a service page that earns relevant impressions but few clicks | Record the exact URL, copy or technical change, owner and live date | Query mix, impressions, clicks and CTR over a sensible comparison period | Qualified calls, forms or bookings where available | Keep, revise, expand or stop—with a reason |

Search Console can help explain search visibility and click-through behaviour when data is examined by page, query and country; it should not be reduced to one ranking screenshot. [Google’s guide to Search Console and Analytics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/google-analytics-search-console) explains how clicks and CTR can be used to spot changes, while their limits still need to be stated in the report.

## Six deliverables to question

1. **A fixed number of blog posts.** Ask which query, reader problem and internal destination each piece supports.
2. **Directory-submission volume.** Ask whether each listing is accurate, relevant and useful to a customer.
3. **Bulk metadata rewrites.** Ask which important pages have a CTR or intent problem first.
4. **Rankings without context.** Ask what pages, queries and business outcomes changed.
5. **“AI optimisation” as a line item.** Ask what reader-facing improvements were actually implemented.
6. **Recommendations without an owner.** A technical issue is not progress until it is shipped or consciously deferred.

There is no useful universal content quota. Google’s people-first guidance explicitly cautions against creating content mainly to attract search traffic or simply to meet a preferred word count. [Read Google’s guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

## Where AEO and GEO fit in a monthly retainer

Answer-engine and generative-search readiness are part of good SEO work when they improve the page for a reader: clear answers, useful tables, accurate entities, primary sources and structured data that matches visible content. They are not a separate guarantee of an AI citation.

Google’s guidance on AI features in Search says the same foundational practices still apply. A sensible monthly report should therefore show the page-level work completed and its limits—not a screenshot presented as permanent AI visibility. [Read Google’s AI-features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide?hl=en).

## Frequently asked questions

### What should monthly SEO services include?

A useful SEO retainer combines prioritisation, implementation, measurement and a documented next decision. The mix changes over time, but the agency should show the URLs changed, why they mattered, evidence observed and unresolved blockers.

### How many blog posts should an SEO agency publish each month?

There is no universal number. Publish or refresh content only where it solves a real search and business problem. A fixed quota is weak if it produces pages with no purpose, ownership or internal-link plan.

### What should an SEO monthly report include?

It should record work shipped, affected URLs, search and conversion signals, limitations, risks and the next priorities. Rankings alone do not explain whether work is helping the business.

### Can an SEO agency guarantee first-page rankings?

No. An agency can commit to research, implementation and reporting, but rankings depend on competition, search systems, site quality and market demand.
