If you find a duplicate Google Business Profile for your Toronto business, do not immediately delete the one you can sign into. First work out whether the two listings represent the same real-world operation, who controls each one, and which details customers currently use. PPC Guru's checklist helps owners make that diagnosis before risking a lost review history or a confusing map presence. If the problem is a suspended profile rather than a duplicate, use our separate [Google Business Profile suspension guide](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/google-business-profile-suspended-toronto).

**The short answer:** Save both profile links and business details, confirm whether they describe the same eligible business at the same location, check management access, and follow Google's current duplicate or ownership process. Do not create another profile as a workaround. If the listings represent two genuinely separate eligible businesses, a merge may be the wrong solution.

## First, are these really duplicates?

Two listings with the same trading name can mean several things: an old location and a current one; an owner-managed listing and an automatically surfaced listing; two profiles for the same storefront; or separate practitioners or departments that may have their own eligibility rules. A map screenshot alone cannot settle which.

Record the profile URLs, visible name, address or service area, phone, website, category, review count and management status. Then ask what a customer would believe each listing represents. Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) generally call for one profile per business; the rules also describe exceptions for genuinely separate locations, departments and individual practitioners.

### A simple evidence table

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Same real business? | Two brand names or legal entities might not be one duplicate. |
| Same real location or service operation? | Old, virtual and current locations raise different issues. |
| Who manages each listing? | A profile you can edit and a profile you cannot edit may need different ownership steps. |
| Which details are accurate? | Wrong phone and website information can misdirect customers. |
| Are reviews attached? | Do not assume reviews will transfer without checking Google's current process. |

## If it is one business, what do you do?

Read Google's live [duplicate-profile and ownership guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/12756178?hl=en) for the route matching your situation. The steps differ depending on whether you control both profiles and whether Google has marked one as a duplicate. Keep evidence of the real-world business, avoid conflicting edits while the case is open, and document every support interaction.

There is a temptation to “solve” duplicate listings by deleting a profile from your own Google Account. But removing your management access is not necessarily the same as removing the public listing. Check what the action actually does before clicking. Likewise, do not invent a new location or add city keywords to the business name to make two profiles appear distinct; Google's rules require accurate real-world representation.

For a GTA service-area contractor, one operation may serve several cities without needing a profile in each. Google's [service-area guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) explains when a service-area business uses one profile and how service areas are represented. A genuine separately staffed location is a different case and should be evaluated on its facts.

## What should you do while waiting for resolution?

Keep your website's contact and location details accurate. Make sure the main contact page, service pages and active profile point to the real business. If calls are reaching an old number, make that a priority customer-service issue, not only an SEO issue. Monitor messages and reviews without promising that a merge, removal or reinstatement will happen on a fixed date.

If the remaining profile has poor category or service information, fix that after you establish which profile should represent the business. ## When should you ask for help?

Bring a specialist in when ownership is disputed, the business has moved, a legitimate practitioner or department profile is involved, or the wrong public phone number is costing enquiries. Ask the specialist to state the exact profile, evidence, proposed Google support route and expected risk before changes are made.
