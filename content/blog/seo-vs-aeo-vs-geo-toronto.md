SEO vs AEO vs GEO is not a choice between three unrelated marketing channels. For a Toronto service business, the sensible order is to make important pages discoverable and relevant, improve how they answer and support buyer questions, then measure search visibility, AI citations and qualified leads together. PPC Guru created the five-layer audit below to help owners find the first broken layer before buying another disconnected service.

The practical recommendation is one integrated search-visibility plan. PPC Guru's [SEO and local search service](/services/seo) covers the foundation; answer and citation improvements should build on that foundation rather than compete with it.

> **Short answer:** Start with SEO fundamentals when priority pages cannot be crawled, indexed or matched to the right service and location. Add AEO-style answer clarity and GEO-style evidence once those pages are genuinely useful. Measure every layer, but do not confuse an AI screenshot with a stable ranking or a qualified lead.

## Quick decision: what should you fix first?

| What you observe | First priority | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Priority pages are blocked, duplicated, missing from the index or technically unreliable | Discoverability | A system cannot reliably retrieve a page it cannot access or process |
| The site has one generic page for several services or locations | Relevance | Better formatting cannot repair a missing or mismatched page |

## SEO vs AEO vs GEO in plain English

The industry does not use these labels with perfect consistency. Treat them as useful viewpoints, not three laws of physics. A useful SEO vs AEO vs GEO comparison should explain the order of work, not merely define the terminology.

### SEO: make the right page findable and competitive

Search engine optimization covers the work required to make a website accessible, understandable, relevant and useful enough to earn visibility in search results. That includes technical health, page architecture, service and location relevance, internal links, content quality, local business information and authority.

SEO still matters when a result appears inside a generated answer. Google's current guidance says its generative AI features use core Search ranking and quality systems, and that existing SEO practices remain relevant. Google also describes AEO and GEO as industry terms; from Google's perspective, improving visibility in its generative search experience is still SEO ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide?hl=en)).

### AEO: make a useful answer easy to understand

Answer engine optimization usually refers to making an accurate answer easier to identify and use in a featured snippet, voice response, AI-generated answer or another direct-answer experience.

In practice, that can mean:

- answering the main question early;
- using descriptive headings;
- explaining conditions and exceptions;
- presenting comparisons in an accessible table; and
- keeping the answer consistent with the rest of the page.

This is not permission to turn every paragraph into a robotic 40-word “answer capsule.” Good answers can be short or detailed. The length should follow the reader's decision.

### GEO: make the source and entity easier to verify

Generative engine optimization usually focuses on whether a generative system can retrieve, interpret, mention or cite a source while composing an answer. Common work includes evidence quality, clear entity information, original data or experience, consistent public facts and technical crawler access.

That does not create a reliable formula for being cited. Different platforms use different retrieval systems, source sets, crawlers and interfaces. A model can answer the same prompt differently over time, by user, by location or after a product update.

### What SEO vs AEO vs GEO actually share

All three reward a useful page that can be reached, understood and trusted. Their difference is mainly the lens:

| Lens | Primary question | Useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| SEO | Can the right page earn visibility for the right search intent? | Indexing, rankings, impressions, clicks and qualified organic outcomes |
| AEO | Can an answer surface identify a clear, accurate response? | Extracted answers, answer-feature appearances and accurate coverage of buyer questions |
| GEO | Can a generative system retrieve and responsibly use the source or brand? | Citations, cited pages, grounding queries, referral traffic and answer accuracy |

The categories overlap enough that buying three separate content programs can create duplicate work, competing pages and confused measurement.

## What should a Toronto business fix first?

Fix the first broken layer, not the newest acronym.

For most owners, the useful SEO vs AEO vs GEO question is not which label wins. It is which observable failure comes first.

A Toronto contractor whose service pages are missing from Google does not need a weekly ChatGPT prompt report first. A clinic with inaccurate practitioner or location information does not need more “quotable” claims. A professional-service firm with strong organic visibility but vague explanations may be ready for answer and evidence improvements.

The order depends on the current site:

| Current situation | Lead with | Add next | Defer for now |
|---|---|---|---|
| New site or serious indexing problems | Technical SEO, canonical URLs, internal links and priority-page inventory | Service/location relevance | Large prompt-monitoring subscription |
| Healthy site with thin or generic pages | Intent mapping and substantive service content | Direct answers, comparisons and evidence | Dozens of prompt-variation pages |
| Strong organic traffic but weak AI visibility | Citation-ready evidence, clear entities and crawler review | Stable prompt benchmark and referral tracking | Rebuilding pages only to change formatting |
| AI mentions but few qualified leads | Landing-page clarity, offer fit and conversion measurement | Citation quality and message consistency | Celebrating mention counts without business context |
| Multi-location business with conflicting public facts | Location ownership and business-profile consistency | Local service content and reviews process | Broad national GEO campaign |

This order protects a limited budget. It also reduces cannibalization: one strong page should own one meaningful intent instead of several near-duplicate pages chasing slight variations of the same question.

## The PPC Guru Five-Layer Search Visibility Stack

Use the stack from the bottom up. A later layer cannot reliably compensate for an earlier failure.

## Layer 1: Discoverable

Can search and answer systems access the correct page?

Start with three checks:

1. Priority pages return a usable response and are not unintentionally blocked.
2. The pages are indexed and eligible to appear with useful snippets where the platform requires that.
3. Internal links, canonical tags and the sitemap point to the intended versions.

Google says a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet before it can be eligible for Google's generative AI features. Eligibility still does not guarantee crawling, indexing or appearance.

Other platforms have their own access controls. OpenAI advises publishers who want content included in ChatGPT search summaries and snippets not to block `OAI-SearchBot` ([OpenAI publisher guidance](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12627856-publishers-and-developers-faq)). Perplexity documents `PerplexityBot` for surfacing and linking websites in its search results ([Perplexity crawler documentation](https://docs.perplexity.ai/docs/resources/perplexity-crawlers)).

Allowing a crawler only removes one possible obstacle. It does not promise a citation or recommendation.

## Layer 2: Relevant

Does a useful page exist for the service, location and decision?

Check whether:

1. every priority commercial intent has a clear URL owner;
2. location and business details match the real operation; and
3. the page resolves the buyer's question without duplicating other pages.

For example, a GTA renovation company might need distinct pages for kitchen renovation and basement finishing because the buyer, proof and scope differ. It does not automatically need 25 pages that repeat the same paragraph for every neighbourhood.

Google warns against creating content for every possible fan-out or query variation primarily to manipulate Search. A larger page count does not make a site more useful. Build the minimum set of distinct pages that accurately represents the business.

## Layer 3: Answerable

Can a busy reader—and an answer system—understand the response without guessing?

Check whether:

1. the main answer appears early;
2. headings, tables and lists follow the reader's decision order; and
3. limitations and next steps are explicit.

Answerability is not the same as brevity. A direct answer can say “it depends” when it immediately explains what it depends on. A Toronto SEO page discussing timelines, for example, should identify the relevant variables: current site health, market competition, content and authority gaps, location coverage and implementation speed.

A good extractable passage retains its meaning when separated from the page. It does not omit the condition that makes the claim honest.

## Layer 4: Citable

Why should another system—or a person—trust this source?

Check whether:

1. material claims connect to primary or permissioned evidence;
2. authorship, method, review date and limitations are visible; and
3. the brand's services, locations and ownership facts are consistent across public sources.

The strongest improvement is often information gain: an original decision framework, transparent calculation, permissioned first-party observation, useful tool, named expert review or well-documented local example.

Do not manufacture that evidence. An AI-generated statistic, unverified case study or synthetic quotation makes a page easier to quote but less trustworthy.

Google's generative-search guidance emphasizes valuable, unique and non-commodity content. It specifically warns against recycled material that could have come from anyone. For PPC Guru, that is why this article provides an actual diagnostic stack and scorecard instead of repackaging definitions.

## Layer 5: Measurable

Can the team distinguish visibility from business value?

Check whether:

1. Search Console and Bing Webmaster baselines are recorded;
2. a stable set of representative buyer prompts is tested with clear caveats; and
3. organic and AI signals connect to qualified enquiries without exposing personal or sensitive information.

Measurement should separate:

- indexed pages;
- search impressions and clicks;
- AI citations or mentions;
- referral sessions;
- enquiries;
- qualified enquiries; and
- booked or won outcomes where the business can measure them appropriately.

Those are different events. A citation may produce no click. A click may produce no enquiry. An enquiry may not fit the service or budget.

## Five AI-search tactics you can ignore

A disciplined SEO vs AEO vs GEO plan also removes work that sounds sophisticated but does not solve the reader's or website's problem.

### 1. “You need llms.txt for Google AI visibility”

Google says it does not use `llms.txt` or other special AI files for Google Search. Maintaining one for another service is a separate decision; it does not improve or harm Google visibility by itself.

### 2. “Every paragraph must be broken into tiny AI chunks”

Google says there is no requirement to chunk content into small pieces. Use paragraphs, headings and tables because they help people navigate—not because a fixed fragment size unlocks AI results.

### 3. “Rewrite the website in a special language for AI”

Clear writing helps. AI-only writing is not required. Preserve nuance, examples and caveats instead of reducing every page to repetitive definitions.

### 4. “Install special GEO schema”

Google says there is no special structured data required for its generative AI features. Continue using supported structured data when it accurately represents visible content and supports an eligible Search feature. Do not add fictional entities or invisible claims.

### 5. “Create a page for every prompt and buy mentions everywhere”

Scaled prompt pages can create duplication and weak content. Inauthentic mentions create another trust problem. Build pages around real buyer decisions, then earn relevant coverage and discussion through useful work.

Google's complete list and qualifications are in its [generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide?hl=en). These statements concern Google Search; other systems may publish different crawler or publisher controls.

## An illustrative Toronto service-business diagnosis

Consider a Toronto HVAC company with a modern homepage, six real services and three service areas.

The site ranks for its brand but has one general “Services” page. Each city appears only in the footer. The blog answers broad maintenance questions, but the articles have no author, sources or link to the relevant service. The owner checks ChatGPT manually and saves screenshots when the brand appears.

The first task is not to commission 100 GEO articles.

The stack would likely identify:

- a relevance gap because distinct services lack useful page owners;
- an internal-link gap between educational and commercial content;
- a citable-source gap because authorship and evidence are unclear; and
- a measurement gap because screenshots are not connected to enquiries.

The sensible sequence is to map real service and location intent, strengthen the necessary pages, connect related content, add truthful authorship and evidence, verify crawler/index access, then establish repeatable measurement.

This scenario is illustrative. It is not a PPC Guru client case study and does not predict a ranking or lead result.

## How to measure AI search visibility without fooling yourself

### Google Search Console

Use the data Google provides for the site and check the exact documentation available in the account. Google's current guide directs site owners to its Generative AI performance reporting for supported Search experiences. Continue reviewing ordinary page/query performance, index coverage and the URLs that actually attract qualified users.

### Bing Webmaster Tools

Microsoft's AI Performance public preview reports total citations, average cited pages, sampled grounding queries, page-level citation activity and trends across supported experiences ([Microsoft Bing Webmaster Blog](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview)).

Microsoft explicitly says those measures do not indicate placement, ranking, authority or a page's role in an individual answer. Treat them as evidence of citation activity, not an AI position report.

### Analytics and referral traffic

OpenAI says ChatGPT search referrals include `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, which can help identify some inbound traffic when analytics is configured correctly. Also inspect other recognizable referral sources and landing pages.

Referral traffic is incomplete by design: many AI answers produce no click, apps may handle referrals differently and privacy or analytics settings can affect attribution.

### A stable prompt benchmark

Create 15–30 representative prompts based on real buyer stages: diagnosis, comparison, cost factors, provider selection, local fit and implementation. Record platform, account state if relevant, location, date, exact prompt, response, sources and whether the answer is accurate.

Repeat the same set monthly. Do not change prompts until the chart looks better, and do not call one run a ranking.

### Qualified outcomes

Add an intake field or sales-review process that distinguishes suitable from unsuitable enquiries without collecting unnecessary sensitive data. Ask:

- Did the person mention an AI assistant or answer?
- Which landing page did they use?
- Did the enquiry match the service, geography and workable budget?
- Did it become a booked conversation or customer?

AI visibility matters commercially only when it improves discovery, trust or the path to a qualified outcome.

## A practical 30-day sequence

### Days 1–5: establish the baseline

- List the priority services, locations and decision-stage questions.
- Assign an existing or planned URL to each meaningful intent.
- Export current Search Console and Bing Webmaster data.
- Record the representative prompt set without treating it as a performance guarantee.

### Days 6–10: repair discovery

- Test response codes, robots controls, canonical tags, rendering and internal links.
- Confirm the intended pages are submitted and eligible for indexing.
- Review crawler access for platforms the business wants to support, including WAF or bot-protection conflicts.

### Days 11–17: improve relevance

- Strengthen priority service and location pages.
- Consolidate thin or overlapping content.
- Link educational pages to the relevant service decision.
- Align core business facts across the website and business profiles.

### Days 18–24: improve answers and evidence

- Put the direct answer near the start where it helps the reader.
- Add comparisons, steps, examples and caveats.
- Replace unsupported claims with primary sources or remove them.
- Add truthful author, method and review information.

### Days 25–30: verify measurement

- Recheck indexing and rendered pages.
- Annotate the baseline and changes.
- Review citations, grounding queries and referral landing pages where available.
- Connect reporting to qualified enquiries and operational next steps.

## Eight questions to ask an SEO, AEO or GEO provider

1. **How do you define each service?** Ask for specific work, not an acronym.
2. **What will you diagnose before creating content?** The provider should inspect crawling, indexing, overlap, business facts and conversion paths.
3. **Which recommendations come from platform documentation, and which are your tests or opinions?** Both can be useful when labelled honestly.
4. **How will you prevent duplicate pages and service-page cannibalization?** More pages are not automatically more visibility.
5. **What original evidence or utility will the content add?** A generic AI summary is not information gain.
6. **How will you measure citations, referrals and qualified leads separately?** One dashboard number should not collapse different events.
7. **What can you not guarantee?** A responsible provider will not guarantee rankings, AI mentions or lead volume.
8. **Who owns the website, content, accounts, exports and prompt benchmark?** The business should retain access and understand what happens when the engagement ends.

Be cautious when a tool or agency implies access to Google's internal ranking data or guaranteed AI placement. Google says third-party tools do not have its internal ranking data and cannot guarantee performance ([Google's third-party SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/third-party-seo?hl=en)).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is SEO dead because people use AI answers?

No. The SEO vs AEO vs GEO debate sometimes hides the shared foundation. Google's generative AI features use core Search systems, and the platform says SEO best practices remain relevant. Search behaviour and click patterns are changing, but discoverability, relevance, technical health and trustworthy content still underpin visibility.

### Are AEO and GEO the same thing?

They overlap heavily, and publishers use the terms differently. AEO commonly emphasizes direct-answer use; GEO commonly emphasizes retrieval, mentions and citations in generated responses. Do not buy separate programs until the provider explains a real difference in work and measurement.

### Which should a Toronto small business prioritize first?

Start with the earliest weak layer. If important pages are not indexed or do not match real service/location intent, prioritize SEO foundations. If those pages are healthy but vague or unsupported, improve answerability and citability. Add measurement throughout.

### Does a website need llms.txt?

Not for Google Search. Google says `llms.txt` does not help or harm visibility in its Search systems. Another service may publish its own controls, so evaluate the intended platform rather than treating one file as universal.

### Is schema markup required for AI citations?

Google says no special structured data is required for its generative AI features. Use supported schema when it truthfully matches visible content and has a legitimate Search purpose. Schema does not replace useful content or evidence.

### Should we create a separate page for every long AI prompt?

Usually not. Group prompts by the underlying decision and build the strongest page for that intent. Separate pages are justified when the audience, service, location, evidence and next step are genuinely different—not because the wording changes.

### How long does AI-search optimization take?

There is no responsible universal timeline. Discovery, indexing, content review, third-party coverage, platform updates and buyer demand all affect visibility. Ask for milestones the provider can control: completed fixes, indexed pages, verified evidence, tracked citations/referrals and qualified-outcome review.

### Can an agency guarantee a ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude or Perplexity mention?

No. An agency can improve crawlability, content quality, evidence, entity consistency and measurement. It cannot control every model, source set, prompt, user context or future response.

### How should we use this scorecard?

Score one priority service path with marketing, technical and sales owners present. Record evidence for every score and assign the first serious failure. Repeat after the repair. Do not use the total as a certification or public performance claim.
