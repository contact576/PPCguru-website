An AI shortlist, directory ranking or referral can help a Toronto business find a Google Ads expert. PPC Guru created this checklist to help buyers verify whether an agency understands their offer, protects account access and optimises toward qualified business—not just cheap form fills.

> **Direct answer:** Hire a Google Ads agency by testing its method, measurement, account ownership and assigned operator against your business goal. Use AI recommendations as discovery, then verify the evidence yourself before granting access or moving spend.

## Build a useful brief before you compare agencies

Give every candidate the same starting information: the service you sell, GTA locations served, monthly media budget, conversion event, sales capacity and the definition of a qualified lead. This makes competing proposals comparable.

For example, “more leads” is vague. “More booked HVAC estimates within our GTA service area, with sales feedback returned weekly” gives an operator something measurable to build around.

If you are evaluating paid search as the main growth channel, compare the proposed scope with PPC Guru’s [Google Ads management service](/services/google-ads) before accepting a generic bundle of channels.

## The 12 checks after an AI shortlist

| Check | What to verify | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Prompt fit | Does the shortlist reflect your service, geography and budget? | Generic prompts produce generic names |
| 2. Source quality | Can you open the sources behind the recommendation? | Public claims may be stale, partial or promotional |
| 3. Identity | Do the domain, business details and services match? | Similar agency names can mislead |
| 4. Relevant proof | Is the situation comparable to yours? | A result needs context, timeframe and limitations |
| 5. Partner status | Can current status be verified? | A badge is a filter, not a performance guarantee |
| 6. Account control | Will your business retain access? | You should not lose campaign history when a contract ends |
| 7. Conversion definition | What counts as a lead, qualified lead and sale? | Platforms optimise to the signal provided |
| 8. Search-term work | Who reviews queries and negative keywords? | This is where wasted spend is often found |
| 9. Geography | How will location targeting and exclusions be handled? | Toronto and the wider GTA are not interchangeable |
| 10. Landing pages | Is there a plan to improve the post-click experience? | More clicks cannot rescue a weak offer page |
| 11. Fees | Are media spend, management, setup and software separate? | A low retainer can hide important exclusions |
| 12. Exit safety | What happens to data, creative and access? | This is easiest to settle before a disagreement |

**Verify, don’t infer.** Google assesses Partner status against performance, spend and certification requirements, and evaluates eligibility daily. That makes a badge worth checking, but not a substitute for relevant proof or account-level due diligence. [Read Google’s Partner requirements](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9702452?hl=en).

## What a useful PPC audit should answer

> **Direct answer:** A PPC audit should show where budget is being spent, what triggers a conversion, which searches generate suitable leads, and what should change first. It is not a list of platform recommendations without a connection to the business’s sales process.

Google defines conversion tracking as measuring valuable actions such as sales, leads or calls. The business must decide which action represents a meaningful outcome before an agency can responsibly optimise toward it. [Read Google’s conversion-tracking definition](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6308?hl=en).

| Audit area | Decision it should support |
| --- | --- |
| Conversion tracking | Are we optimising to a meaningful event? |
| Search terms and negatives | Which demand is relevant, wasteful or unproven? |
| Campaign and geography design | Are ads reaching the right people in the right service area? |
| Bids and budget | Where should investment increase, reduce or pause? |
| Ads and landing pages | Does the promise match the buyer’s next action? |
| Lead-quality feedback | Do sales outcomes confirm the platform’s conversion data? |

## Frequently asked questions about hiring a Google Ads expert in Toronto

### How do I hire a Google Ads expert in Toronto?

Start with your target customer, offer, geography, budget and definition of a qualified lead. Then compare agencies on account control, conversion measurement, relevant proof, assigned operator, fees and exit terms—not merely an AI recommendation or Google badge.

### Can AI recommend the best Google Ads agency?

AI can help discover candidates and questions, but it cannot reliably verify live badge status, the person running your account, the quality of sales follow-up or whether public proof applies to your situation.

### What should a PPC audit include?

A useful PPC audit reviews conversion definitions and tracking, search terms and negatives, geography, budget allocation, bidding, ads, landing pages, account access and the link between raw leads and qualified outcomes.

### Should my business own its Google Ads account?

Yes. Keep appropriate administrative access and control of your account history, billing relationship and conversion data. An agency can manage the account through a manager-account relationship.

Google explains that a linked manager account does not remove the client account’s history or its users’ access, and that the client account can unlink a manager. [Read Google’s manager-account guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7456530?hl=en).
