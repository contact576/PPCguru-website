Keyword cannibalization becomes a real business problem when two PPC Guru-style service pages—or two pages on your own Toronto business site—try to answer the same buyer decision and neither makes the next step clear. Two URLs appearing for one query are not automatically broken. PPC Guru's test helps owners decide whether to keep, differentiate or consolidate them. If you are still mapping the overall site, our [monthly SEO services checklist](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/monthly-seo-services-toronto-checklist) explains why page ownership belongs in ongoing SEO work.

**The short answer:** Check whether the pages share the same *intent*, not merely the same phrase. Compare the query, pages, offer, audience and next action. If each page helps a different buyer question, keep and clarify them. If they are nearly interchangeable, choose one primary owner, combine genuinely useful material when appropriate, and plan redirects or canonicals according to the actual URL and content relationship.

## What is a sign of a genuine conflict?

Consider a Toronto agency site with a “Toronto SEO services” page and a new blog post titled “Best SEO services Toronto.” Both pitch the agency and use the same CTA. The blog may compete with the service page instead of supporting it. A useful blog question—“What should the first 90 days of SEO include?”—has a different job: help the buyer assess a plan, then link to the service page for the commercial choice.

That example describes a hypothetical site, not a measured PPC Guru result. The diagnostic point is the same for a contractor with “bathroom renovation Toronto” and “Toronto bathroom remodel” pages that repeat the same scope, photos and city claims.

## The five-column page-ownership test

Create a small sheet with one row per candidate page:

| URL | Main query/intent | Audience and offer | Unique evidence | Intended next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core service page | Hire a provider | Buyer ready to compare or enquire | Scope, fit, proof, process | Consultation or quote |
| Supporting guide | Diagnose or learn | Buyer checking a problem or approach | Checklist, example, method | Read service page if help is needed |
| Location page | Confirm local availability | Buyer in a real service area | Distinct local details | Local enquiry |

Now ask: if you swapped the titles, would the two pages still read almost the same? If yes, they may not have distinct jobs. Also inspect Search Console by query and page over a useful period. Alternating impressions may be a clue, but not proof: search results can vary by context, and both pages might validly appear for different search intents.

## Keep, differentiate, merge—or investigate a technical duplicate?

**Keep** both when they solve clearly different questions. Strengthen headings, internal links and examples so a reader can tell the distinction without guessing.

**Differentiate** when the pages have distinct potential but currently repeat each other. Give the commercial page the service decision and the guide the educational decision. Avoid splitting every close keyword variation into another thin page.

**Merge** when two pages truly cover the same task and one strong resource would serve people better. Preserve worthwhile sections and route old URLs carefully after checking inbound links, user bookmarks and analytics. Google's [redirect guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/301-redirects) describes redirects when a removed URL has a relevant new home.

**Investigate canonicalization** when the pages are duplicate or very similar URL versions, such as parameters or printer variants. Google's [canonical guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls) explains how signals differ. A canonical tag is not a magic fix for two genuinely different pages that are poorly planned.

If the page is missing from Google for another reason, see our separate [indexing diagnosis for Toronto websites](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/google-not-indexing-website-toronto). Do not label every indexing or CTR issue “cannibalization.”

## Which URL should own a service keyword?

Start with the page that best completes the buyer's task. For “SEO agency Toronto,” that is usually a service or agency page with scope, process, proof and a contact path—not a long glossary post. For “how to tell if two service pages compete,” a guide can be more useful. The live results for the query should confirm the intent rather than a keyword tool deciding by volume alone.

Then review internal links. Do existing pages point the commercial phrase to the commercial page? Do related guides explain the next step without repeating the service pitch? Google says descriptive internal links help people and search systems understand destination pages ([link guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable)).

Before creating another page from a competitor keyword export, use our [SEO competitor-analysis guide for Toronto service businesses](/blog/seo-competitor-analysis-toronto-service-businesses) to separate a genuine customer-task gap from a phrase your existing page may already own.

## A caution before deleting anything

Do not delete a page just because a tool shows two URLs for one phrase. The less-visited page may rank for valuable long-tail questions, assist enquiries, or be the only useful explanation for a segment. Check query-level evidence, referral links and actual user need. If you merge, test the destination and redirect rather than sending every old URL to the home page.
