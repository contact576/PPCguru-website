A 90-day SEO plan should not be a promise that your Toronto business will rank first by the end of the quarter. It should tell you which pages and leads matter, what is stopping them, who will make the changes, and how you will decide what to do next. PPC Guru built this decision plan for owners who need to judge an SEO proposal before committing to months of work. If you are still comparing providers, start with our guide to [what monthly SEO services should include](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/monthly-seo-services-toronto-checklist); this article focuses on the *first* three months.

**The short answer:** Spend the first 30 days establishing a trustworthy baseline and fixing the biggest blockers. Use days 31–60 to improve the pages closest to qualified enquiries. In days 61–90, verify what shipped, read early search and lead signals, and decide the next investment. There is no universal traffic or ranking milestone that every site should hit by day 90.

## Before the calendar starts: define a lead worth winning

Imagine an Etobicoke contractor who serves Toronto and Mississauga. The site records 30 contact forms, but half ask for work outside its service area. “More forms” is not the objective. Qualified project enquiries from the right locations are.

That example is illustrative, not a PPC Guru client result. It points to a useful opening question: **Which service, location and customer type would make this SEO work commercially worthwhile?** Write the answer before choosing blog topics. A clinic might count appointment requests for a specific service. A B2B firm might track demo enquiries from businesses in its target market. The lead definition changes the plan.

## Days 1–30: find the constraint, not just the warnings

Month one should end with a short, ordered worklist—not a 60-page export of every tool alert.

1. **Confirm measurement.** Check that the site can distinguish calls, form submissions and booked outcomes where technically and legally appropriate. Separate spam and irrelevant requests from qualified enquiries.
2. **Map the pages that should win.** Identify the main service and location pages, their intended searches, and the next action each asks the visitor to take.
3. **Check access and visibility.** Use Search Console and a focused crawl to find important pages that cannot be indexed, wrong canonicals, broken internal paths or outdated redirects. Google notes that technical eligibility does not guarantee indexing or rankings; fix the problems affecting priority pages first, not every warning indiscriminately ([Google Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials)).
4. **Inspect the actual buying path.** On a phone, can a prospect see the service area, understand the offer, trust the evidence and make contact without friction?

If the site has a serious indexing issue, the first month may be almost entirely diagnostic and technical. If the foundation is healthy, it may focus on a high-intent service page instead. The right sequence depends on the site you have, not a template.

**Ask for this deliverable:** a one-page baseline showing the priority URLs, the reason each matters, the problem found, the proposed change, the owner and the verification method. If the agency cannot distinguish “recommended” from “implemented,” the plan is not ready.

## Days 31–60: ship the work closest to revenue

This is the month to move from findings to changes. For a Toronto service business, that often means making the core service pages more useful before producing several broad blog posts. Clarify who the service is for, where it is available, what happens next and what evidence supports the choice. Add relevant local proof only if it is real and permissioned. Link closely related explanations to the service page instead of making a second page fight for the same “SEO agency Toronto” or “contractor near me” query.

The implementation list should be selective. A business with broken contact forms needs those fixed before extra traffic. A company with several near-identical GTA city pages may need consolidation or differentiation before another location page. A clinic with strong service pages but missing practitioner information may need a different sequence altogether.

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) recommends logical organization, readable useful content and descriptive links. Those are sensible standards for the work, but the benefit comes from applying them to the right pages and checking the result—not from ticking boxes.

**Ask for this deliverable:** a change log with before/after URLs, live dates, remaining dependencies and a note on what each change was meant to improve.

## Days 61–90: read signals without inventing a success story

Search Console may show new impressions before clicks. A page can improve for one query and fall for another. Seasonality and local competition can obscure a short period. Three months is enough to see whether the team has measured correctly, shipped meaningful work and learned from it; it is not enough to guarantee a revenue outcome.

Review four layers in order:

| Layer | Question at day 90 | Next decision |
|---|---|---|
| Implementation | Did the agreed changes actually go live? | Finish or correct incomplete work. |
| Search visibility | Are the right pages appearing for relevant queries? | Improve the page/search match or investigate indexing. |
| User action | Are suitable visitors calling, booking or enquiring? | Fix the offer, proof or contact path if needed. |
| Lead quality | Do enquiries match the service, geography and budget? | Refine targeting and qualification, not just traffic volume. |

For the difference between search growth and business return, see PPC Guru's guide to [measuring ROI from SEO services](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/how-to-measure-roi-from-seo-services). Search Console and analytics do not count the same thing in the same way, so do not blend their numbers into one unlabelled “traffic” figure ([Google's Search Console and Analytics guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/google-analytics-search-console)).

## A quick test for a proposed 90-day SEO plan

Give one point for every answer your proposed agency can show you in writing:

- [ ] The target service, geography and qualified-lead definition are explicit.
- [ ] The most important existing pages and their intent are identified.
- [ ] Technical and content work is prioritized by likely business impact, not tool alert count.
- [ ] An implementation owner and verification step sit beside each proposed change.
- [ ] Month two includes changes to ship, not only research and reporting.
- [ ] Day-90 reporting separates shipped work, search signals and lead outcomes.
- [ ] The plan states what would cause priorities to change.

This is a review aid, not a scoring system that predicts SEO performance. If a proposal misses several items, ask for a clearer plan before buying more deliverables.

## What should you *not* expect after 90 days?

Do not expect a guaranteed first-page ranking, a fixed number of leads, or a reliable answer to every long-term ROI question. Search results change and competitive terms can take longer. Also be cautious when a contract treats articles published as the principal outcome. A useful first quarter leaves a defensible baseline, implemented improvements and a better next decision—even if the traffic graph has not yet become dramatic.
