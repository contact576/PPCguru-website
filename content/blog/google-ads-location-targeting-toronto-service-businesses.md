A Toronto campaign can name the right service and still attract enquiries from places the business cannot serve. Before changing bids or adding more keywords, inspect the campaign's **location targeting**. The useful boundary is your real service area: where your team can fulfil the work, handle calls and book appointments.

**Short answer:** List the municipalities or neighbourhoods you actually serve, compare that list with every campaign's targets and exclusions, check its advanced location option, then review geographic performance alongside qualified leads. Google says its default advanced option includes people who are in, regularly in, **or interested in** a targeted location; targeting is based on signals and is not perfectly accurate ([Google Ads Help: geographic targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453995?hl=en)).

## Start with a service area map

Write down the places you can serve without making a promise your operations cannot keep. A hypothetical HVAC company based in Etobicoke might cover Toronto and Mississauga but not every address in York Region. The campaign should reflect its own dispatch rules, not a generic definition of the GTA.

For each service line, record:

| Decision | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| Core area | Where jobs are routinely accepted and scheduled |
| Conditional area | Places served only for particular jobs or minimum project sizes |
| Outside area | Places you cannot fulfil or would normally decline |
| Landing page promise | Whether the page names the same area the campaign targets |
| Intake result | Whether the CRM records the customer's actual job location |

This is an operating exercise before it is an Ads setting. If your booking team does not record the job location, a location report alone cannot tell you which enquiries were serviceable. See our [guide to tracking qualified leads and booked jobs](https://ppcguru.ca/blog/google-ads-lead-quality-tracking-booked-jobs) for a practical lead-stage framework.

## Check targets, radius and exclusions campaign by campaign

Open the campaign's **Locations** settings and compare each target with your service map. Google lets advertisers add named locations and target a radius around a point; its setup instructions say to inspect the map before saving a radius ([Google Ads Help: set up location targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10835274?hl=en)). A radius is simple, but it may include places across a lake, border or travel corridor that your team does not serve. Review the actual map, not just the number of kilometres.

An illustrative Toronto renovation campaign might target Toronto, Mississauga and Vaughan separately because the company accepts projects in all three. If it handles only large projects in Vaughan, that is a reason to consider a distinct campaign or landing page with an accurate offer, rather than assuming all areas will produce equally suitable leads.

Record exclusions as well. They can help prevent unwanted reach, but do not treat them as a substitute for defining the target area correctly. After every edit, verify the location list and the ad's service-area wording. A targeted city and a landing page that says “Canada-wide” send conflicting signals to prospective customers.

## Choose the advanced location option deliberately

Google describes two useful targeting choices in campaign settings: **Presence** and **Presence or interest** ([Google Ads Help: campaign settings](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1704395?hl=en)). Under Google's default, a person outside Toronto who searches for a Toronto service may be eligible because they have shown interest in Toronto. That can be useful for some businesses. A same-day local repair service may have a stronger reason to focus on people in or regularly in its target area.

Do not assume that switching to Presence solves every out-of-area enquiry. Google states that location signals vary and its targeting is a best effort rather than a guarantee ([Google Ads Help: geographic targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453995?hl=en)). Google's guide to reducing clicks from outside targeted areas explains where to select Presence in the campaign's location options ([Google Ads Help: out-of-area clicks](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9376662?hl=en)).

Make the choice based on the service. A clinic seeking patients near its office and a contractor willing to work with an out-of-town property owner may reasonably use different settings. Document the reason so a later campaign audit can evaluate the choice.

## Read the geographic report with lead outcomes

Once the campaign has run, inspect the **Locations** report. Google distinguishes **Targeted locations**, which reports performance for selected targets, from **Matched locations**, which reports where ads appeared based on physical location or location of interest ([Google Ads Help: measuring geographic performance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453994?hl=en)). Review both views before deciding that a city target is working or failing.

Compare clicks and cost with the job addresses and lead stages in your CRM. For example, a campaign might receive many clicks associated with a nearby city, but only a few enquiries from homes the team can reach. That is a prompt to inspect the search terms, campaign settings, ad text and intake records. It is not proof that geography alone caused the problem.

Use a consistent weekly review:

1. Export or note each campaign's targets, exclusions and advanced location option.
2. Compare the matched-location report with the service area map.
3. Count qualified leads and booked jobs by **job location**, where the CRM captures it.
4. Investigate areas with meaningful spend and poor fit before excluding them.
5. Check that ad copy and landing pages accurately describe the places served.
6. Record changes and compare later results with enough time for leads to move through the sales process.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a Toronto service business target all of the GTA?

Only when it can reliably serve and follow up with customers across that area. Start with actual coverage, then adjust using location and lead-quality data. A broad target is not automatically better.

### What is the difference between Presence and Presence or interest?

Presence focuses on people in or regularly in targeted locations. Presence or interest can also reach people who show interest in those places. Google describes both in its [geographic targeting guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453995?hl=en).

### Can location targeting guarantee every click is local?

No. Google says location targeting uses several signals and does not guarantee complete accuracy. Check geographic reports and the customer's actual job location, and correct settings when the evidence warrants it.
