A Toronto business hiring a Google Ads agency usually pays two separate bills: ad spend paid to Google and a management fee paid to the agency. Setup, tracking or landing page work may cost extra. The useful question is: What will the entire program cost, and can it acquire customers profitably?

Use the published examples below to understand fee models, then request a proposal for your account.

## The short answer: budget for the full program

Your monthly outlay is ad spend plus the management fee, with any separately billed setup, tracking or landing page work added on. Ask every agency to show those amounts on separate lines.

For example, C$3,000 in monthly ad spend plus a C$750 management fee equals C$3,750 per month, before separately billed work. This is an illustration, not a PPC Guru quote or a recommended minimum budget.

## How much should you spend on the ads themselves?

Start with the economics of a customer. Estimate how many new customers you can serve, how many qualified enquiries become customers, and the most you can afford to pay to acquire one.

Suppose a service business wants 10 new customers in a month and closes one in four qualified leads. It needs about 40 qualified leads. If its planning target is C$75 in ad spend per qualified lead, the working ad budget is C$3,000. Add management and any agreed setup costs to get the full budget.

That is a planning scenario, not a guarantee. Test your assumptions with PPC Guru's Google Ads calculator at https://ppcguru.ca/tools/google-ads-calculator, then replace estimates with campaign data.

## Why do agency quotes differ?

Account condition. A new account needs research, structure and tracking. An existing one may need targeted fixes. Ask what happens in month one and afterward.

Geography and services. The proposal should name the services, markets and campaign types it covers.

Conversion measurement. Ask how calls, forms, bookings and customers will be measured. A form fill is not necessarily a qualified lead.

Landing pages. Find out whether the agency will review the page, recommend edits or build a new one.

Reporting and ownership. Confirm who manages the account, what you will see and who owns the account and data.

## What a useful proposal should show

A proposal should identify the campaigns the agency will manage and the work it will do in the first 30 days. Look for a clear explanation of how it will review search terms, exclude irrelevant traffic, test ads and adjust budgets. Ask which decisions need your approval and how often you will discuss results.

The proposal should also explain its measurement plan. If phone calls are important, how will they be recorded as leads? If customers usually book later, will the agency be able to connect the original enquiry to the booking? A monthly report of clicks and form submissions is less useful when your real goal is booked work.

Compare proposals using the same scope. One agency may include tracking and landing page work while another quotes those separately. Write down the first-month total, the ongoing monthly total and any minimum commitment before deciding which offer costs less.

## Judge the fee against acquired customers

Include the management fee when calculating acquisition cost. If you spend C$3,000 on ads and C$750 on management and acquire 10 customers, the combined cost is C$375 per customer. Looking only at ad spend would show C$300 and understate what you paid.

Track enquiries through to booked jobs or customers. Compare the combined acquisition cost with what those customers are worth to your business.

## What if the agency charges a percentage of ad spend?

Ask whether the percentage has a minimum monthly fee. For example, 15% of C$3,000 is C$450, but a C$750 minimum means you would pay C$750. At C$10,000 in ad spend, the same 15% model would charge C$1,500. These are arithmetic examples, not PPC Guru prices.

A percentage fee is easier to evaluate when you calculate it at your current budget and a realistic higher budget. Ask what additional work the agency provides as spend rises.

## Seven questions to ask before signing

1. What is my expected monthly ad spend, management fee and one-time cost?
2. Is there a minimum fee or contract term?
3. What campaign work happens each month?
4. Are conversion tracking and landing page work included?
5. Who will manage the account and explain the results?
6. What counts as a qualified lead, booking or customer?
7. Who owns the account and data when the agreement ends?

If your offer or lead follow-up process is not ready, address that before increasing spend.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the agency fee include ad spend?

Do not assume it does. PPC Guru separates management fees from ad spend, which clients pay directly to Google. Ask every provider for an itemized quote.

### Is a flat fee better than a percentage of ad spend?

Either model can work. Calculate what you would pay at your current budget and at a higher budget. Compare the minimum fee, included work and any changes in scope.

### Can an agency guarantee leads or sales?

An agency can set targets and explain its assumptions, but results depend on demand, competition, the offer and how enquiries are handled. Ask how it will measure and review outcomes.
