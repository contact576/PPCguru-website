When **website traffic is up but leads are down**, buying more traffic is usually the wrong first move. The leak is often in measurement, query intent, location fit, the service page, the enquiry path or follow-up. PPC Guru's [Toronto SEO service](/services/seo) connects Search Console, analytics and real lead outcomes so a rise in visits is judged by the business it creates—not by a green traffic chart.

Start with one comparison period and one channel. “All traffic” can hide a drop in qualified organic visitors behind a spike from referrals, bots, job seekers or low-intent articles.

## The short answer

Traffic and leads move in opposite directions when the new visitors are less qualified, high-intent pages lose visibility, tracking changes, calls or forms break, the offer no longer matches the search, mobile friction grows, or leads arrive but are mishandled. Diagnose the funnel in this order: **measurement → traffic mix → landing page → enquiry action → qualification → follow-up**.

## First, prove that leads are actually down

Before changing titles or calls to action, reconcile three sources:

1. **Google Search Console:** organic impressions, clicks, queries, pages, countries and devices.
2. **GA4 or another analytics platform:** landing sessions and meaningful events.
3. **CRM, call records and inbox:** real enquiries, qualified opportunities, booked outcomes and revenue.

Google Analytics calls actions important to the business **key events**. A form confirmation, booked consultation or qualified call can be measured, but the event must fire once and at the right point. A button click alone may not prove a successful enquiry.

Use the same dates, time zone and page scope. Annotate campaign launches, consent changes, redesigns, phone-number changes and seasonality. If analytics says 50 leads and the CRM has 18, measurement is part of the problem.

## A four-box diagnosis

| Traffic quality | Conversion path | Likely problem | First action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right visitors | Working | Follow-up, qualification, seasonality or reporting lag | Audit CRM and response process |
| Right visitors | Broken/weak | Page, form, phone, proof or UX | Test the full enquiry journey |
| Wrong visitors | Working | Query, content or geography mix | Inspect pages and queries that grew |
| Unclear visitors | Unclear | Measurement | Fix tracking before optimizing |

Do not skip the bottom-right box. A confident answer built on broken tracking sends the team in the wrong direction.

## 1. Check whether the traffic increase is real

Look for:

- internal staff traffic;
- bots or referral spam;
- a tagging change that started counting twice;
- a consent banner that changed what is recorded;
- a channel recategorized as direct or referral;
- a date-range or time-zone mismatch.

Compare users, sessions and landing pages rather than one total. If the increase exists in analytics but not in Search Console clicks, it may not be organic growth.

## 2. Find which landing pages gained traffic

Ten thousand visits to a calculator, job post or broad definition page do not replace 100 visits to a high-intent service page.

Group landing pages into:

- commercial service and location pages;
- decision-support articles;
- broad informational content;
- careers, tools, support and irrelevant URLs.

Calculate the change for each group. If informational growth masks a loss on service pages, the headline “traffic is up” is technically true and commercially misleading.

## 3. Inspect the queries behind the growth

In Search Console, compare query clicks and impressions for the pages that grew. Label each query:

- ready to hire;
- comparing options;
- diagnosing a problem;
- learning generally;
- irrelevant to service, geography or audience.

SEO can bring the wrong visitors when content targets volume without buyer fit. A Toronto agency should not celebrate traffic from people seeking free templates in another country if the goal is local retainers.

## 4. Separate geography and device

A page may gain mobile traffic while its mobile form fails. It may grow internationally while losing Toronto clicks. It may perform in one GTA municipality and fall in another.

Compare country, city where reliable and privacy-appropriate, and device. Search Console geographic data and analytics do not use identical definitions, so look for consistent direction rather than forcing the totals to match perfectly.

## 5. Check whether high-intent visibility fell

Overall traffic can rise while money-page visibility declines. Review the queries and pages closest to an enquiry:

- service + city;
- service + near me;
- cost, quote or consultation queries;
- urgent problem queries;
- branded searches that often follow earlier research.

Check impressions, clicks, average position and search-result changes. A new map pack, ad block, video result or AI answer can change click behaviour even when a ranking tool looks stable.

## 6. Re-read the landing page as a buyer

The visitor should be able to answer within seconds:

- Is this service for my situation?
- Do you serve my location?
- What happens after I contact you?
- Why should I trust the business?
- Is the action easy on a phone?

If the page opens with agency slogans, hides the service or sends every visitor to the homepage, it creates friction. Improve the promise and decision path before adding more keywords.

For paid-landing-page issues, use PPC Guru's separate guide to [landing-page mistakes that hurt conversions](/blog/landing-page-mistakes-killing-conversions). This article focuses on the broader SEO-to-lead diagnostic.

## 7. Test every call, form and booking route

Complete the journey on iPhone-size and Android-size screens, on Wi-Fi and mobile data, and in an incognito session.

Test:

- tap-to-call numbers;
- form validation and error messages;
- submission confirmation;
- email or CRM delivery;
- calendar availability and time zone;
- CAPTCHA and consent behaviour;
- thank-you page or success event;
- sticky buttons, overlays and chat widgets.

A visually successful submit can still fail in the backend. Confirm the lead appears where the team works it.

## 8. Inspect trust at the decision point

Traffic may grow after a content push while conversion falls because visitors reach a thin or inconsistent offer. Place truthful proof close to the action:

- real service details and scope;
- visible location or service-area information;
- relevant qualifications or licences where applicable;
- verifiable reviews and case evidence;
- clear privacy and contact information;
- honest limitations and next steps.

Do not invent results or use generic badges. For healthcare, immigration and other sensitive services, accuracy and professional review matter more than an aggressive promise.

## 9. Compare the offer with the searcher's stage

A reader asking “how long does local SEO take?” may not be ready for a 12-month contract. A contractor searching for emergency lead-generation help may need a direct audit or call.

Match the action:

- early research → checklist or useful guide;
- active diagnosis → audit or assessment;
- vendor comparison → scope and consultation;
- urgent service need → phone or booking route.

One hard sell on every page can lower response even when the content is useful.

## 10. Review lead quality, not just lead count

Define a qualified lead using fields the sales team can apply consistently: needed service, location, budget range, timeline and decision authority. Avoid collecting sensitive or unnecessary information.

Then separate:

- spam;
- vendors and job seekers;
- out-of-area requests;
- service mismatches;
- viable but unbooked opportunities;
- booked or quoted opportunities.

If total leads fell but qualified leads held steady, the situation is different from a true pipeline decline. If total leads rose but qualified leads collapsed, traffic growth is not the win it appears to be.

## 11. Audit speed-to-lead and offline handling

The website does not control what happens after the enquiry. Check:

- missed-call rate and voicemail handling;
- response time by channel;
- weekend and after-hours coverage;
- duplicate or lost CRM records;
- whether staff know which services and areas qualify;
- follow-up attempts and booking friction.

A conversion problem can live entirely outside the page. PPC Guru's [speed-to-lead guide](/blog/speed-to-lead-why-follow-up-fast) helps teams inspect this handoff without pretending more traffic will repair it.

## What should a weekly dashboard show?

Keep it small enough to use:

| Layer | Metric | Diagnostic question |
|---|---|---|
| Search demand | Impressions by priority query group | Are the right searches available? |
| Visibility | Clicks and landing pages | Are high-intent pages attracting visits? |
| Experience | Form/call completion and errors | Can a visitor finish the action? |
| Qualification | Qualified leads by service/location | Are the enquiries commercially relevant? |
| Operations | Contacted, booked, quoted | Is the team handling them? |
| Outcome | Customers/revenue where trackable | Is the channel contributing to business? |

Do not force a single “healthy conversion rate” onto every service. The denominator, action, traffic mix, price and buying cycle differ. Establish your own clean baseline, then compare like with like.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can SEO increase traffic but reduce leads?

Yes. That can happen when growth comes from low-intent, out-of-area or irrelevant queries while high-intent pages lose visibility, or when conversion and tracking problems occur at the same time. SEO did not necessarily “cause” the decline; the page and query breakdown identifies what changed.

### Should I publish more content when leads are down?

Only if the diagnosis shows a missing question or service need. If forms are broken, commercial pages lost visibility or current articles attract the wrong audience, more publishing can increase the wrong metric.

### Which report should I check first?

Start with the system closest to truth: CRM and call records for real enquiries, GA4 for landing behaviour and key events, and Search Console for organic queries and pages. Reconcile them rather than choosing the most positive chart.

### Could seasonality explain the change?

Yes. Compare year over year when possible and use Google Trends or business records to examine demand. Seasonality can coexist with tracking, page or sales-process issues.

### Do AI answers mean SEO traffic is no longer useful?

No. Search-result features can change click behaviour, but that does not make qualified organic discovery irrelevant. Track visibility, branded demand and assisted journeys alongside clicks, while keeping the website capable of converting visitors who do arrive.
