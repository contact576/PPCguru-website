## The invisibility problem is bigger than it looks ## 

Start with the hard number. A 2026 study by the agency Victorious tested 177 brands across five industries, including healthcare, SaaS, and financial services. They ran customer-style questions through eight AI tools (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google's AI Overviews, Copilot, Claude, and more) and logged more than 107,000 answers.

Only 18 of those 177 brands showed up at all. The other 89.8% were invisible.

That catches people off guard, because the marketing press keeps insisting the AI race is already lost if you're not in it. The data says the opposite. In most categories almost nobody has been surfaced yet, and by one 2026 estimate, 84% of brands aren't even monitoring how AI tools describe them.

The field is close to empty. Move now and you're competing against a handful of early names in your category, not the whole market. In my experience, windows this wide do not stay open long.

One caveat up front, so you calibrate expectations. AI answers aren't fixed. In one analysis, only about 30% of brands stayed visible across back-to-back answers to the same question.

So the goal isn't to appear in every answer every time. It's to raise the odds that you're named when it counts, then keep raising them. Treat it like a batting average, not a light switch.

## Why this got urgent for a Toronto business specifically ## 

Two years ago, "will people research my business through a chatbot" was hypothetical. Not anymore.

More than 900 million people now use ChatGPT every week, and it crossed a billion monthly users in 2026. On the Google side, [AI Overviews](https://search.google/intl/en-IN/ways-to-search/ai-overviews/)(the AI summary above the blue links) now appear on roughly half of tracked searches. BrightEdge put that near 48% in early 2026, up 58% year over year, though estimates vary by method.

The shift is fastest exactly where it hurts a local service business. BrightEdge found AI answers grew from 36% to 82% of B2B technology queries in a single year, with similar jumps across service categories.

Picture the actual moment. Someone asks "who's the best physiotherapist in Leslieville" or "top commercial contractor near me," and the assistant answers with a short list of names instead of a page of links. If you're not on that list, you don't lose the click. You never entered the conversation.

Now the part most owners miss, and the reason I put it ahead of vanity metrics. The traffic AI sends is not low quality.

Several 2026 analyses found that visitors arriving from AI tools convert at multiples of ordinary search traffic, around four times higher, and for service businesses higher still. The mechanism is obvious once you've seen it. By the time someone clicks through from an AI answer, the tool has vetted the options and the person has half-decided.

So being invisible doesn't just cost you exposure. It quietly removes you from your best-converting source of new business, with no bounced visit to show for it.

And often there's no click to catch at all. In one study, about 93% of AI search sessions ended without anyone visiting a website, which makes the assistant's answer the whole sales moment.

### First, check whether you're actually invisible
### 

Before you fix anything, confirm the problem. Ten minutes is enough.

Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity three or four questions the way a customer would. Not "what is [your company]," but "who are the best [your service] companies in [your neighbourhood or city]." Try a few phrasings, because the same question can produce a different answer each time.

Note three things: whether you appear, whether your competitors appear, and which websites the AI names as its sources.

That third one is where the strategy hides. The sources it cites are the pages doing the work, and nine times out of ten they're review sites, directories, and roundup articles, not the businesses' own homepages.

A business can be doing fine on Google and be nowhere in AI, because the two systems pull from different places. I've seen accounts sitting on page one of Google that appear in zero AI answers. Different game, different board.

### The counterintuitive lever: reviews
### Heading

The most useful finding in this space came from a separate study by Seer Interactive, which analyzed more than 800,000 AI responses to work out what actually gets a brand cited.

Brands with no [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ppcguru.ca)review profile had a median AI citation rate of about 1%. Functionally invisible.

Brands with even a bare-bones profile, as few as 1 to 13 reviews, showed up in roughly 53.5% of relevant answers. That's a 52 percentage point jump, from almost nothing to more than half, off a handful of reviews.

The reason is simple, and it's the same reason review sites have quietly run local buying decisions for over a decade. Assistants won't vouch for a business on their own judgment, so they lean on sources that already carry trust and rank well in search.

Seer found about 99.5% of the Trustpilot citations they saw arrived through ordinary organic ranking. The platform's own authority does the heavy lifting, and you inherit it just by having a profile there.

The effect shows up fast, often within weeks. You're not gaming anything. You're handing these tools something credible to point at.

### The 3-step fix
### Heading

**Step 1: Get a real review profile live**

Claim and fill out a Google Business Profile and a Trustpilot profile, then ask your last ten happy clients for a review this week.

You're not chasing a flawless five-star average. You're crossing the line from "no profile" to "has a profile," which is where the bulk of the gain sits.

Two details separate the owners who get results from the ones who don't. Keep reviews coming steadily rather than in one burst, because freshness counts and a profile that stalled a year ago reads as stale. And if you already have Google reviews but nothing else, add one more platform your industry trusts, so assistants can corroborate you in more than one place.

**Step 2: Get named on pages that aren't yours**

A 2026 analysis, found that roughly 85% of the brand mentions AI tools produce come from third-party pages, not the company's own website. Assistants trust what other people say about you more than what you say about yourself, and they cite it accordingly.

So go earn those mentions: local business directories, industry associations, "best [service] in Toronto" roundups you can often earn a spot in just by reaching out, a quote in a local publication, and relevant community threads.

Reddit deserves its own line. In AI's study of 30 million citations, it was the single most-cited domain across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews. A genuine, helpful presence in the right subreddits is now a visibility play, not a waste of an afternoon.

Because these are pages you don't control, they carry more weight with an assistant than any sentence on your own site.

**Step 3: Make your own site easy for a machine to read**

Your website still matters. Just not the way it did five years ago. The job now is to let an AI work out who you are, what you do, and where you operate without guessing.

The mechanics are well documented. BrightEdge found that sites adding structured data and FAQ blocks saw a 44% increase in AI citations, pages updated within 60 days were about 1.9 times more likely to appear, and content with clear author information was roughly three times more likely to be cited.

In plain terms: give the tools a clear, plain-language "about" section, a services page that names your city and the neighbourhoods you cover, and the same business name, address, and phone number everywhere they appear. Add an FAQ page that answers real customer questions in full sentences.

Behind the scenes, structured data (the hidden labels that tell a search engine "this is a business, this is its rating, this is its location") helps them read you correctly. Hand that piece to someone technical. It's a one-time setup, not an ongoing cost.
